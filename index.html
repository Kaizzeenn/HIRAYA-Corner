Project story

A safe space built around real student needs.

HIRAYA means hopes and aspirations. The project responds to the need for safe expression, anonymous communication, emotional awareness, and a stronger sense of belonging.

1

Safe space

Students need support when facing stress, peer pressure, neglect, and fear of sharing concerns.

2

Anonymous expression

The Freedom Box and Board allow students to communicate without fear of judgment.

3

Emotional awareness

Mood check-ins and reflection help students understand emotions and choices.

4

Supportive community

Activities and student leaders encourage connection, participation, and belonging.

Project Goal

Provide a safe and student-friendly space where students can freely share feelings, thoughts, and concerns while feeling heard, supported, and valued.

✓

Establish one HIRAYA Corner

Complete the corner during the NSTP deployment period.

✓

Train at least 10 student leaders

Prepare leaders to assist with future activities and maintenance.

✓

Collect weekly feedback

Use the Freedom Box as a confidential feedback channel.

✓

Conduct values-based activities

Promote reflection, expression, peer support, and personal growth.

The approved approach focused on coordination, establishment of the HIRAYA Corner, student orientation, confidential monitoring, and project turnover for continuity.

Narrative description or nature of the place

SRDKC is a non-profit organization dedicated to helping poor children, youth, and families. It focuses on developing long-term capabilities rather than providing only short-term aid, so individuals and communities can direct and sustain their own growth.

Programs, Services, and Beneficiaries

Programs and services offered by the center

SRDKC provides educational support and long-term capability-building programs for children, youth, and families. It began by assisting children from the Smokey Mountain community and later expanded to provide day care services for children ages four to six.

Description of the beneficiaries served by the center

The center serves poor children, youth, and families. For Project HIRAYA, the direct beneficiaries were the Junior High School students of the SRD Konkokyo Learning Center, together with student leaders, teachers, guidance personnel, and the school community.

Summary

Challenges Encountered

☀️

Heat During the First Deployment

The warm venue affected the comfort and energy of both the facilitators and participants. The group adjusted the pacing, allowed short pauses, and prepared the materials before each activity to avoid longer delays.

⏱️

Limited Time

Several activities had to be completed within the deployment period. Tasks were assigned to specific facilitators, transitions were shortened, and the group prioritized the most important parts of the program.

₱

Budget and Materials

The group had to manage the cost of the board, Freedom Box, art materials, prizes, and other supplies. Members compared prices, prioritized essential materials, shared available resources, and avoided unnecessary purchases.

💡

Brainstorming and Finalizing Ideas

Members suggested different activities, designs, and approaches. The group combined the strongest ideas, divided responsibilities, and checked that the final plan remained aligned with the partner center’s needs and the approved Program of Action.
